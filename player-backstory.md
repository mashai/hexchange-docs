# Player Backstory

This is the story behind why the player made a bargain with the Traveler (see
[game-overview.md](game-overview.md)) and what they're actually doing with the shop, underneath
the shop management. It's the source material for [the-village.md](the-village.md)'s "Personal
Mystery" — that section covers the reveal *mechanic* (which NPCs ask, what sharing unlocks); this
doc covers what's actually being revealed.

None of this is told to the player upfront. It surfaces gradually, in fragments, through pub
conversation — see the-village.md for how.

---

## The Sister

When the player was young, her older sister disappeared. The player's memories of it are
fragmented and unreliable — she was a child. What she does hold onto, clearly, is that her
sister was taken by something supernatural. Rescuing her sister is the player's defining want,
the thing everything else in the game is in service of.

---

## The Bargain

The player went to the Traveler and asked him to rescue her sister directly. He told her he
couldn't. He's not permitted to say why — this refusal is a significant clue, not a throwaway
line, and it should not be explained or lingered on when it first happens. Its weight only
becomes clear much later (see The Truth, below).

What he offered instead: the means to rescue her sister herself. All the knowledge of the world,
and the worlds beyond, in exchange for running the shop. That's the actual shape of the bargain
from the player's side — the shop isn't the point, it's the price of access to what she actually
wants: a way to find and save her sister.

This sits alongside, and doesn't resolve, the existing "the price of the bargain is never
revealed" line in game-overview.md's opening narrative. Knowledge-for-shopkeeping is the visible,
stated part of the deal. Whether there's something else underneath it is still open.

---

## Fae and Demons Are the Same Thing

World-building rule, not yet stated anywhere else in the docs: fae and demons are the same
creatures, described through two different cultural lenses. "Demon" is the Christian framing;
"fae" is the older one. Both refer to beings native to another dimension, who can be called into
this world — and who trade in bargains and favors with the humans who call them.

This isn't common knowledge in-world. Most villagers treat fae and demons as separate folklore
categories (consistent with how they're already used as two distinct, both-wrong red herrings in
[mystery-crop-field.md](mystery-crop-field.md) — that's the ordinary, uninformed view, and nothing
here contradicts it). That they're the same thing is a conclusion the player has to arrive at
through her own research, not something any character just tells her.

---

## How the Player Gets There

Over the course of the game, through books and bestiary knowledge, the player rules out the
mundane supernatural explanations one at a time — it wasn't other witches, wasn't trolls, wasn't
any of the ordinary things that live in these woods. By elimination, she concludes whatever took
her sister has to have been fae or demon.

In parallel, her own memory of the disappearance surfaces in pieces, unlocked through pub
conversation as specific NPCs get close enough to ask real questions (the mechanic for this is
spec'd in the-village.md's Personal Mystery section — this doc is the content that mechanic is
drip-feeding).

These two threads — the external research and the internal memory — are meant to converge, not
run in parallel forever. The player should end up piecing together the "fae/demon took her"
conclusion from her own investigation at roughly the same time her recovered memories start
lining up with it.

---

## The Truth

Two reveals, late-game:

**The Traveler is fae — or demon, same thing.** Everything he's said and done gets
re-read in light of this. In particular, his refusal to rescue the sister himself stops looking
like an arbitrary rule and starts looking like something he was bound by.

**What actually happened to the sister.** She wasn't taken. She was going to be married off to a
man she didn't want to marry, and asked to leave — into the fae realm — to escape it. She fell in
love with the fae she made that bargain with, and stayed. Not a kidnapping. A choice.

The rule that makes both of these fit together: fae/demons who make a deal with a human are bound
to honor it, and cannot interfere with an existing deal already in place — including one made by
another of their own kind. That's why the Traveler couldn't just go get her: the sister's own
bargain was already standing, and breaking or overriding it wasn't something he was able to do,
whatever he might have wanted. It's also, presumably, why he could offer the player a path to
finding out herself instead of just refusing outright — that much wasn't off-limits.

## Open Questions

Not decided yet, flagging rather than guessing:

- Does the player ever find or reunite with her sister? Is there an ending, or a late-game scene,
  built around that — or does the story end at the realization itself?
- Exact trigger conditions for each memory-fragment reveal: which specific NPCs, gated on what
  (friendship tier, story progress, a particular book)?
- Sequencing: does the player learn the Traveler is fae before or after she learns the truth
  about her sister, or can it happen either order depending on play?
- Is "the Traveler" the same fae the sister ended up with, a different one, or is that left
  ambiguous?
- Does any of this connect to game-overview.md's unrevealed "price of the bargain," or is that a
  separate mystery this doc doesn't touch?
- Where does this fit in scope.md's Need/Want/Polish tiers? Not yet classified there.
